## Why the middle bullet isn't a regional-variant line

**Italian is the first single-variant source.** Every release since v3.1 filled the 🗺️ slot
with a real pair from the shipped regional card — *voiture / char*, *ônibus / autocarro*.
Italian has no such card: `regionalVariants.js` carries `records: []` for `it` **by decision**,
because Italy↔Switzerland lexical divergence is thin and Italian's real variation is dialectal
rather than a clean national binary. Inventing a pair would break the rule's own "regional
examples come from the shipped card, never invented."

So the slot carries the next most shareable *true* thing, and v3.4 has an unusually good one:
the phonetics explanations were re-anchored from English to Italian, and **224 of 1,756**
changed as a result. The Japanese line is not a flourish — it is what the shipped card now
says, because Japanese really does have five pure vowels, geminate consonants and a tapped *r*
that an Italian speaker already owns. Until this release the app told them the opposite.

**For v3.5 German the 🗺️ slot comes back** — German has a real Deutschland/Österreich/Schweiz
split (*Semmel/Brötchen*, *Obers/Sahne*).

## Accuracy — what this post may claim, and why

| Claim | Backing |
|---|---|
| "explanations included" | **New in v3.4; the previous two posts were forbidden from saying it.** `it` reads 16,700/16,700 explanations, 7,220/7,220 wrong notes, 19,464/19,464 distractor notes — 100%, matching `es`. pt and fr were backfilled to 100% in the same release. Measured by `scripts/verify-l10n-coverage.mjs` through the real render path. |
| "the same five vowels as Italian" | Shipped content in `jaForEn.js`'s fono bank, from the v3.4 L1 anchor pass. |
| "13 languages, each a full course" | `tracksForNativeLang("it")` returns 13, every one ≥300 bank items (`scripts/_it-parity-audit.mjs`, 22/22). 13 is the **most of any source language** — Italian loses only one track to its own target, where French and Portuguese each lose two. |
| "the whole app follows" | True at the level it was for French: menus, Settings, onboarding, sign-in, subtitles, answer choices, explanations, Word Bank, chips, sublabels, Help and About. |

**Still not true, so still unsaid:** the *Grammar Gym* page and some status/error screens are
English for everyone. The video tour is English (its in-app badge says so).
